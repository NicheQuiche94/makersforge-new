The Affiliate Program

A partner growth manual. How the program works, who we want in it, what you say, and how you get paid for building it.

MakersForge Limited Version 1.0 Reference this before every partner conversation

Before you start

This is our standard set, not scripture

Everything in here is what we currently believe works, written down so all three of us are doing the same thing and so nobody has to reinvent the pitch on a Tuesday afternoon. Follow it, and you will not go far wrong.

But we deliberately overindex on exploration over exploitation. If you find a better way to open a conversation, a segment we have not thought about, an objection we handle badly, or a step that is wasting your time, bring it to Andre. Not as a complaint, as an improvement. These processes get reviewed constantly and the people running them are the ones best placed to see what is broken.

The parts that are genuinely fixed are few, and they are marked where they appear: the commission rate, the pricing, and anything involving a promise to a partner or a client. Everything else is open.

Why it's written this way

You do the best work when you have real input into how it gets done. That is the whole point of writing this down rather than managing you through it. Use the parts that help, challenge the parts that don't, and tell us which is which.

01 — Why this is the strategy

Four channels, ranked. Not four channels, split.

A business can advertise four ways: paid ads, content, cold outbound, and referrals. Most split their effort evenly across all four and end up mediocre at each. We rank them instead, and the ranking decides where your hours go.

Referrals & affiliatesPrimary. The channel we intend to own outright. This manual is that channel.
Cold outboundSecondary. A recruitment business cannot ignore it. It is how we reach studios nobody has introduced us to.
ContentOptional. Worth doing when we have something worth saying. Never at the expense of the first two.
Paid adsWe don't. B2B acquisition costs run to roughly £3,000 per customer. Spending that to earn £12,500 makes no sense while cheaper channels are nowhere near exhausted.

The goal for the primary channel is specific: build it until it produces more work than we can handle, which forces us to hire more recruiters. That is why this program is not a side project or a lead-gen experiment. It is the growth engine, and building it is the job.

04 — The offer

One sentence. Say it exactly like this.

“Every deal we do that came from your introduction pays you £1,000.”

No tiers. No ladders. No qualification thresholds. Ten deals from your intros is ten thousand pounds. That is the entire commission structure, and its simplicity is the point.

We ran tiers before, at £750 rising to £1,500. They were removed on purpose. Tiers create admin, arguments about qualification, and confusion in exactly the conversation where you need to be clear. Do not reintroduce them, and do not imply a partner could negotiate a higher rate.

Why individuals get nothing to sell

This is deliberate and worth understanding, because you will be asked. When an individual passes on a discount or an offer, they stop sounding like a friend making a recommendation and start sounding like they are working an angle. The trust is the asset. Anything we hand them to sell erodes it.

An agency passing on an offer is just doing business. Same mechanic, completely different signal.

06 — The agency hook

Something valuable they can give away for free

An agency partner needs a reason for their client to want the introduction. So we give them a deliverable they can hand over at no cost to themselves, which makes them look generous and well-connected, and delivers us a qualified lead.

A 45-minute session with the founder of MakersForge

The full name, and how to say it: a one-to-one Pre-Recruitment Brief Overview and Interview Process Health Check, direct with Andre. Free to the client, free to the agency, and not something we advertise publicly.

What happens in the 45 minutes

Process health checkWe map their current interview process, stage by stage, and say honestly where it is costing them people. Six stages over three months is why they keep losing candidates at offer, and most founders have never had anyone tell them that.
Brief buildWe work through the role with them: level, what it owns and does not own, must-have against learnable, realistic salary band.
SpecificityThe part they feel immediately. Most job ads bring everyone to the table. We show them how to write one that only attracts the handful of people they actually want.
Written follow-upThe brief, the process recommendations, and the interview scorecard for that discipline, sent afterwards. Complete and usable whether or not they ever hire us.

Why this and not something else

Two rules govern every giveaway we design. It cannot contain candidates, because anything that puts talent in front of a company for free competes with the fee we exist to earn. And it has to be something they cannot do alone, or it is a PDF.

A health check passes both. It contains no candidates, only judgement, and judgement is the one thing a founder cannot download.

Who qualifies

Funded or profitably trading, 10 to 100 people, with a named role they intend to fill within 90 days. The constraint does your lead qualification for you, and it lets the agency say “I can get you one of these” rather than “here's a link.”

Andre runs these personally

Every session, without exception. It is a founder-to-founder conversation and that is a large part of what makes it feel valuable. You still earn your override and any fill commission exactly as normal. Book it in, brief Andre on the context, and hand it over.

Never say this

Do not describe the session as access to candidates, a shortlist, or CVs. It is advice on their process and their brief. Promising talent for free undercuts the only thing we sell.

08 — Who we don't

The one group to walk away from

Media-buying agencies that manage client spend. Do not sign them, however well connected they are.

The reason is structural, not personal. When a studio hires a capable in-house UA manager, the case for outsourcing spend management weakens and the agency's retainer is eventually at risk. We would be paying them £1,000 to accelerate their own churn. They will take the agreement, and then quietly never make an introduction.

The nuance that matters

Split the UA world. Agencies selling creative production are among our best partners, because more in-house growth headcount means more creative briefs. Agencies managing spend are structurally against us. Same industry, opposite incentive. Ask which side of that line their revenue sits on.

09 — The filter

Does their business get better when their client's team gets stronger?

One question. Ask it about every potential affiliate before you spend an hour on them. It is faster and more reliable than any list of segments, and it will correctly classify people we have not thought of yet.

Yes

Their incentive points the same way as ours. Referring us is self-interested in the good way. Sign them.

No

No commission fixes a conflicted incentive. They will sign and never refer. Walk away, and keep the relationship warm for something else.

The override runs for twelve months from the date the affiliate signs, on every placement they generate, whether or not you touch the role. One affiliate producing four placements a year pays you £2,500 for work you did once.

Read this honestly

Originating and filling a client yourself pays more per deal (30.5%, or £3,813). The affiliate route pays less per deal but compounds, because each affiliate keeps producing without further effort from you. The two are not in competition. Do both.

11 — The pitch

The lightbulb moment

Do not open by explaining a program. Open by naming something they already do.

“You know when you're talking to someone, they mention a problem, and you get that immediate flash of I know exactly who you should talk to? What if I paid you for those moments?”

That framing works because it is true. You are not asking them to sell, prospect, or change their behaviour. You are asking them to be paid for an instinct they already have.

Then, in order

  1. What we do, in one line

    Specialist recruitment for mobile games and consumer apps. Fixed fee, not a percentage.

  2. What triggers an introduction

    Any time someone mentions they are struggling to hire, sitting on 500 CVs, or losing candidates at offer stage.

  3. What they get

    £1,000 for every deal that comes from their intro. Uncapped.

  4. What it costs them

    An email introduction. We take it from there and they stay informed.

  5. Agency partners only: the giveaway

    A free 45-minute brief and interview process health check with Andre, that they can offer their clients.

12 — Objections

What they will say, and what you say back

“I don't want to look like I'm selling something to my contacts.”

You are not. You make one introduction and step out. There is no pitch to deliver, no follow-up on you, and nothing you have to defend. If it goes nowhere, nothing happens to your relationship.

“What if the hire doesn't work out? It reflects on me.”

Fair, and it is the objection worth taking seriously. We stand behind placements, and the specific guarantee terms are confirmed per engagement. Check the current wording with Andre rather than improvising a promise.

“How long until I actually see money?”

Be honest, because a vague answer here damages trust later. The placement has to happen, the client has to pay, then commission is paid within 14 days. Realistically that is weeks, not days, from the introduction.

“I already know a recruiter.”

Most people do. We are specialists in mobile games and consumer apps, on a fixed fee rather than a percentage of salary. If their contact is a generalist, those are different products.

“Is £1,000 the top rate? Can we discuss it?”

Yes it is the rate, and no it does not move. It is the same for everyone, which is exactly why nobody has to negotiate for it. Do not imply otherwise.

“What if someone else refers the same company?”

First submission wins, with a 90-day window on that company. If theirs is second, we tell them the same day rather than letting them discover it at payout.

What moves and what doesn't

The affiliate commission is £1,000 and does not move. On client pricing you have £1,000 of movement either way on the £12,500 fee, so you are never stuck on a call. Anything beyond that, or any change to payment terms, comes to Andre.

13 — Signing an affiliate

The manual process, for now

The affiliate platform is being built. Until it ships, everything is captured by hand. Capture these exact fields, because they map directly onto the system that is coming and anything missing has to be chased later.

  1. Qualify against section 09

    One question. If the answer is no, stop.

  2. Explain the offer

    £1,000 per deal, uncapped, no tiers. Agency partners also get the health check session to give away.

  3. Send the agreement

    Request it from Andre. Nobody is an affiliate until it is signed.

  4. Record them

    Full name, email, company, individual or agency, segment, who recruited them, signup date. Your name in that record is what triggers your override, so do not skip it.

  5. Brief them on what a good lead looks like

    Funded or profitable, 10 to 100 people, hiring a specialist within 90 days.
