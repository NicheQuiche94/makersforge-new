Before you start

This is our standard set, not scripture

Written down so all of us are doing the same thing, and so nobody has to reinvent it on a Tuesday afternoon. Follow it and you will not go far wrong.

We deliberately overindex on exploration over exploitation. If you spot a better way to run any of this, bring it to Andre. The people running a process are the ones best placed to see what is broken in it.

The fixed parts are few and marked where they appear. Everything else is open.

01 · What it's for

The board is not a product. It's a lead machine.

We run a job board for growth hires at makersforge.gg. It fills two ways: it ingests roles automatically from around 500 tracked companies every day, and companies can post directly for free.

The board itself does not make money and is not supposed to. Its job is twofold. It puts MakersForge in front of companies and talent repeatedly at no cost to them, and it feeds our CRM with a live, enriched list of every company in our market that is currently hiring.

That second part is the one to internalise. Every role on the board becomes a CRM record automatically. You do not build a prospect list. It builds itself overnight, every night.

How to think about it

A job posting is a company telling us, in writing and unprompted, exactly what they are hiring for and when. Finding that any other way costs hours per company. Here it arrives enriched, in the CRM, before you get up.

02 · The slower half

Familiarity does the rest

Nobody buys a £12,500 service from a company they met once. They buy from a company whose name they have seen five times, whose content they have read, and whose board they have already used.

That is the freemium pattern, and it runs on a longer clock than the CRM half. A company that posts with us this month may not be a client for a year. That is fine. The board is buying repeated, cost-free exposure while the CRM does the immediate work.

03 · The loop

Two circuits, one board

The board runs a commercial circuit and a talent circuit at the same time. Each one makes the other worth showing up for.

Ingested daily from around 500 tracked companies, plus free direct postings.

02 CRM populates

03 Talent browses

Growth, UA, monetisation and creative people arrive looking for roles.

04 The roster grows

05 The roster sells

Represented talent is what makes the paid service worth £12,500, and what makes the board worth posting on.

Content feeds the top of both circuits. Every useful thing we publish brings companies and talent to the board, and the loop turns again.

04 · The line

Applicants are free. Candidates are not.

This is the most important idea in this document, and the question you will eventually be asked: if companies can get people from our board for free, why would anyone pay us £12,500?

Because they are not the same people, and it is not the same work.

The board, free
  • Active job seekers who are already looking
  • They apply, the company sifts
  • No screening, no shortlist, no assessment
  • The company does every hour of the work
  • They get applicants
The service, £12,500
  • People who are not looking and will never see a job ad
  • We approach, qualify, and sell the role in
  • Screened, assessed, shortlisted
  • The founder spends hours, not weeks
  • They get candidates

A free board does not cannibalise us, because the senior people worth £12,500 are precisely the ones not trawling job boards. If anything the board proves the point: a founder who posts, receives 300 applications, and finds nobody usable has just experienced our sales pitch without us saying a word.

Use this in conversation

“Post it on our board, it's free. If the right person applies, brilliant, you've saved yourself a fee. If you get 300 CVs and none of them fit, that's usually a signal the person you want isn't looking, and that's the point we're useful.”

05 · Capturing talent

Nobody leaves the funnel

Anyone browsing the board is, by definition, a growth or games professional thinking about their next move. Letting them apply and disappear wastes the most valuable traffic we have.

At the point they click to apply, we capture their email and offer representation. One field, one checkbox, no wall in front of the listing itself.

The offer, roughly

“Want to hear about roles that never get posted? A lot of what we work on is confidential and never reaches a job board. Tick here and we'll be in touch when something fits.”

That is a real offer rather than a data grab, which is why it converts and why it does not annoy people. It also feeds the represented roster, which is the asset the entire paid service rests on.

Placement matters

The capture happens at apply, never in front of the listing. Gating the ads themselves would cut the traffic that makes the board worth posting on, which defeats the purpose.

06 · The CRM connection

Your prospect list builds itself overnight

This is the part that changes how you work. The board and the CRM are wired together, so every role that appears on the board becomes a company record with the data enriched around it.

You are not doing cold outreach in the usual sense. You are contacting companies you already know are hiring, for a role you can name, in a discipline we place. That is not warm, since they have never heard of us, but it is prequalified, and it removes the worst part of outbound, which is guessing who might need you.

What arrives automaticallyRoles from around 500 tracked companies, ingested daily, enriched, in the CRM
What you get from itWho is hiring, what for, and when they started looking
What it replacesList building, guessing, and researching whether a company has a live need
Timing is the edge

A role that has been open a while is a better conversation than one posted yesterday. Early on they are optimistic. A few weeks in, they have seen the applications and the optimism has gone. That is when we are useful.

So the system notifies you at day 10 and again at day 20 on any role still live. Treat those as your first and second check-ins. Reach out earlier if you have a reason, this is a strong suggestion rather than a rule, but the two prompts exist because that is when the conversation tends to land.

07 · Your daily process

What you actually do with it

Two responsibilities. Both are small, both are daily, and the system degrades quickly if either slips.

  1. Check the CRM for new roles, every day

    Overnight ingestion means there is something new each morning. Work it while it is fresh, because so is everyone else's.

  2. Work the day 10 and day 20 lists

    You get a notification of every role still open at those marks. Day 10 is the first check-in, day 20 the second. By day 20 they have usually seen enough applications to know it is not working.

  3. Feed anything the system missed

    If you see a relevant role anywhere else, on LinkedIn, at a conference, in a Discord, and it is not already in the CRM, add it. It flows through to the board automatically. This is how coverage grows beyond the 500 we already track.

Why the backfill matters

Every role you add improves the board for candidates and adds a prospect to the CRM for all of us. Five minutes of yours becomes a permanent asset. Treat it as part of the job rather than admin.

Then use it in conversation

  1. As a cold opener

    “Saw you're hiring a UA lead. We run a growth-specific job board, happy to put your role on it, no charge and no catch.” There is nothing to decline.

  2. As a follow-up with someone not ready

    They are not using an agency this quarter. Fine. Get the role on the board and stay in the conversation while they try it themselves.

  3. As a reason to check back

    At day 10 and again at day 20: “How's it going, getting anyone decent?” If the answer is no, that is the sales conversation, and they opened it.

The mindset

You are not giving away a job ad. You are buying the right to ask how it went.

08 · What we don't do

Rules that protect the funnel

We don't charge for postingsThe moment it costs money it stops being a frictionless opener and becomes a product we have to sell. That is a worse business than the one we already have.
We don't send board applicants to clients as our candidatesThat is a CV-sending service, and it is on our own list of disqualifying client behaviours. If someone applies through the board, they applied to that company, not to us.
We don't promise a company the board will fill their roleSometimes it will. Promising it sets us up to be blamed for a free service, and makes the paid service look like the same thing.
We don't spam people who opted inThe represented roster works because those people trust us. Contact them about roles that genuinely fit, and not otherwise.
We don't let junk on the boardAn unmoderated board fills with irrelevant listings, candidate traffic drops, and the whole loop stalls. Postings get a light review.

09 · What good looks like

What to watch

The board is a funnel, so the useful numbers are conversions between stages, not totals. Volume flatters and tells you nothing.

MeasureWhy it matters
Companies posting per monthThe raw supply of qualified, intent-signalled leads
Repeat postersThe strongest buying signal on the board. A company hiring repeatedly has a growing team and a hiring problem.
